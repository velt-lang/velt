std: a sleep polled or dropped on another worker is handed over by the worker that holds it (#3207)

A sleep lives in the timer block of the worker that first polled it, and only
that worker touches the block. A promise handed to another task is polled by
that task from then on, and dropped by whoever holds it last, on that task's
worker: such a poll wrote the sleep's waker while its worker's expiry read it,
such a drop unlinked it from a list it was not in, and its expiry put it on
the due list of a task of another worker (segfaults in about one run in eight
of the review's probes).

A poll or drop elsewhere now writes nothing in the sleep (sched_sleep_move.velt):
it leaves a request on the holding worker's list and wakes it. The holder
takes the sleep off its list, stores its state last (new again, or seen when
it had fired), and wakes the asking waker and the one the sleep was registered
with; the task polls it again and registers it in its own worker's block. A
dropped one is freed by the holder. Requests under way are counted in the
sleep, so none names freed memory, and the program's end waits for them.

Which task polls is the task the worker is polling, not what the promise's
waker or context says. Another task's poll on the sleep's own worker anchors
that task and makes it the sleep's owner for carries.

In a counted run a sleep that expires for a task of another worker, or is
dropped off its worker, is an `ICE:`.

Closes #3207
