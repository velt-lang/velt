std: gather-back counts inbox takes with nothing else queued; any wake ends a spin; the gather test's actors are anchored (review)

Gather-back: a worker hands its inbox's tasks to worker 0 once 16 takes of
its inbox in a row each found nothing else queued on it, instead of counting
kernel wakes against polls. What counts is the queue, not whether the worker
had parked: a caller's next call may land before the worker reaches its park.
A take with tasks queued ends the run, so a burst's spread stays.

The gather test never passed a task through an inbox: its actors could move,
and a woken task that may move runs in its waker's slot, so its last round
only showed where `main` was (the burst sometimes moved `main`, and then all
16 actors ran with it). The actors now sleep once (anchored: a call from
another worker reaches them through their worker's inbox), the caller is a
task pinned to worker 0, and the burst lasts until a round finds actors away.
With gather-back off the test fails (4 to 11 actors stay away).

The spin is a state of the park word (`PARK_SPIN`): a wake takes the word
down with no system call, and the spin watches the word, so a wake that
brings no task (the last task's end, an exit) ends it too. Before, such a
wake found the word down and was dropped until the next one
(std_scheduler_bounds a_task_ending_last_on_another_worker_frees_its_block_first
failed 8 of 12 under load). `E_SPINNING` is gone. A park turns the spin back
on only when another worker ended it (its own timers and sockets don't).

Inlining: `steal` and `borrow` into the idle loop, as before the offers
carried sleeps, and sched_mem.velt `copy`, which copies every async call's
initial state (await_deep +9.8 % when a unit boundary left it a call).
