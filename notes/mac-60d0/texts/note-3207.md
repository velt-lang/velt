2. First reading of sched_sleep.velt (on #3201's tree), so you can correct me early. The issue's fix ('anchor a task whenever it polls a sleep') covers the case where the second task polls the sleep on the SAME worker whose timer block holds it: anchored there, an offer then carries the sleep with it (carry matches by the waker's task). But a handed-on sleep can also be polled, or dropped, on ANOTHER worker than the one whose block holds it (a fresh task that was given the promise starts on any worker; a movable task runs in its waker's slot). Then, anchored or not:
   - sleepPoll writes S_WAKER on worker E while worker W's expiry may read it;
   - sleepDrop unlinks it from block(t.exec()) = E's block while it is linked in W's list (both lists corrupted);
   - seen() counts TM_UNSEEN down on E's block for a sleep W's block counted up.
   So the rule the code needs is 'a pending sleep is only polled and dropped on the worker whose block holds it', and anchoring at the second poll gives that only if the poll happens there. Options I see: (a) a task that polls a sleep pending in another worker's block is sent home to that worker first (home = that worker, anchored, re-polled there; needs the sleep to record its block, one word, and the requeue after such a poll to go to the home's inbox); a drop off the block's worker is deferred to it (marked, freed by its owner at expiry); (b) atomic states on every sleep (expiry, poll and drop CAS the state), which costs every timer row. I'd take (a) and measure the timer rows. I'll confirm which of these shapes the probes actually reach before writing any of it.
