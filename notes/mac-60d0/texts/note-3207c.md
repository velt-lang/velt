What it does (std/lang/hooks/sched_sleep_move.velt, new; sched_sleep.velt):
- A sleep records the worker whose block holds it (S_HOME). A poll or drop on another worker (or of a sleep an offered task carries) writes nothing in it: it pushes a request on the holding worker's atomic list (E_SLEEP_ASKS, taken whole by its owner, served oldest first) and unparks it.
- The holder takes the sleep off its list (pending, due or fired), stores its state LAST (new again, or seen when it had fired) and wakes the asking waker; the task's next poll registers it in its own worker's block as a first poll does (anchored there). No 'arrived' list is needed: after the state store only the asking task touches the sleep.
- Your five points: (1) in transit a sleep is owned by its holder until that last store; a drop while requests are under way becomes a request itself, and requests are counted in the sleep (S_ASKS) so whoever ends the last one frees a dropped sleep: no request names freed memory, in any serving order; a third worker's poll in transit pushes another request, which finds 'another worker holds it' and goes on to that worker; expiry while queued: the holder serves requests before its timer look. (2) push-only stacks drained whole. (3) the waker clone is consumed by the wake at service. (4) served at the top of park and re-checked after the park word goes up (as the inbox), and at every I/O tick of a busy worker. (5) a worker with requests counts as busy in the end scan (sched_end.velt), so a counted exit only happens with every list empty.
- The second poller on the SAME worker (the issue's original shape) is anchored and becomes the sleep's owner for carries.
- The rule as an ICE in counted runs (VELT_RC_STATS, what the tests use): 'a sleep expires for a task on another worker', 'is dropped on another worker'.
